01 · In brief

One page for buyers, and a tool for sales.

The problem

Buying ERP is a big, risky decision, and partner sites usually describe it in software terms. My first version was dark, and its orange buttons failed contrast checks.

What I did

Ordered the page around the questions a buyer asks, designed it in Figma, exported the code from my designs, and refined it over several review rounds.

Outcome

Live since November 2026: a cleaner, easier-to-read site, shaped around how the sales team pitches. Scores and next steps are in the Outcome section.

My part

Page structure, layout, visual design and the interactive sections for both versions, plus developing version one in Odoo’s editor and the redesign from code exported from Figma.

02 · Challenge

ERP is hard to picture.

The buyers are owners and operations leads at growing manufacturers, retailers and wholesalers. They feel the problem every day, but module names don’t help them see the fix, and every Odoo partner makes similar promises.

How might we

How might we show a business owner what their week could look like on Odoo, before they talk to anyone?

03 · Version one

Version one, inside the brand rules.

Just weeks after I joined, I designed Gravitai’s first site, using the brand colour palette and fonts exactly as they were given to me, and developed it in Odoo’s website editor. It gave Gravitai a site of its own. But clients told us the orange felt harsh against the black, and that the black itself was hard to read. That feedback shaped everything in the redesign.

04 · Audit

Where version one worked, and where it failed.

Clients had told us the orange felt harsh on black, and the black was hard to read. So before redesigning, I checked version one against WCAG 2.2 AA using its real design layers. The dark theme passed on paper, but the orange buttons failed, which matched what clients felt. Then I ran the same checks on the redesign, to see what had really improved and what still needs work.

05 · Direction

A whole new look, not a recolour.

Version one followed the brand book to the letter: dark pages, orange on everything, and mono type in capitals. The redesign kept the brand but changed the whole look. It is cleaner and calmer, with more space, softer shapes and real product screens. Moving to a white base was a team decision. I was told I could use purple, so I showed the team gradients that mixed purple with other colours, and they loved them. The dot pattern was added after that. I also pushed hard for Poppins as the body font, because Tomorrow and IBM Plex Mono were too similar and clashed.

06 · Design system

A small system for a white site.

The redesign runs on two typefaces, four colours and a handful of components, taken straight from my landing page file. Tomorrow carries the brand, Poppins does the reading.

07 · Structure

One scroll, one buyer’s questions.

Before anyone books a call, they have questions. I ordered the page so each section answers one, and it ends on the call itself.

08 · Trust

Proof they can check themselves.

Choosing a partner is a trust decision before it’s a software one, so the proof comes early, and buyers can check it away from our own site.

09 · Show, don’t list

Show the Monday, not the modules.

Two interactive sections do the explaining, so buyers see the result instead of reading a list of modules.

10 · Feedback

Shaped by the people who sell it.

I reviewed each round with the business, and asked the sales team how they’d use the site in their calls with prospects. Reviews pushed the system to be more consistent: one shape language for buttons and blocks, one icon style, and clearer calls to action on every page. Sales asked for real photos of the team instead of AI imagery, and for the “See a project from the inside” video to follow the same story they tell in their pitches.

12 · Build

Designed by me, coded from my designs.

Version one was built section by section in Odoo’s website editor. For the redesign, I exported the code straight from my Figma designs and it went into Odoo, the platform Gravitai sells. Because the code came straight from the designs, the live page matches them closely. The landing page went live first.

I’m tracking these scores over the coming weeks, with a plan for each weak spot.

Less code up front

Defer scripts that aren’t needed on load, and trim about 570 KB of unused JavaScript plus unused CSS.

Faster first paint

Remove render-blocking requests, which cost about 1.9 seconds on mobile.

Accessibility fixes

14 · Options I rejected

Roads I didn’t take.

An idea I tried and dropped, and what replaced it.

✕ Rejected

A giant 3D “G” for the hero

I made these in Higgsfield AI: the G built brick by brick on a building site, then standing over a warehouse, a factory and a loading yard. The team felt it was too AI and too impersonal, so it never went live.

✓ What went live instead

The live hero: a plain promise, and a real sales order moving through Odoo.

Why I let it go

It was my idea and I liked it, but the team was right. People choosing an Odoo partner want to see their own working day, not a giant logo. I kept the sense of movement and put it where it earns its place: a real order moving through Odoo in the hero.

15 · Trade-offs

What I pushed for, and what I let go.

Leading the design meant picking which calls to fight for. These are the ones that shaped the site most.

Pushed for

More colour than orange

I was given purple to work with. Instead of arguing for more, I showed the team gradients built from the brand colours plus a few of my own. They loved them, and the dot pattern was added on top later.

Pushed for

Poppins for body text

Version one used IBM Plex Mono for body copy. Next to Tomorrow it looked too similar and the two clashed, so I really pushed to switch to Poppins. It reads more easily at small sizes, and it went into the redesign.

Let go

My own shipped design

Version one was mine and it was live. Clients said the orange felt harsh against the black and the pages were hard to read, so I redesigned it rather than defend it. Stepping back gave me perspective, and with that user feedback, a fresh direction made sense.

16 · Reflection

Selling a system means showing a day.

I’m proudest that I designed both versions, then got to improve my own shipped work using what clients told us about the first one. Next time I’d check contrast at the very start, so the orange button wouldn’t need a later fix. And I’d plan image sizes for mobile from day one, because mobile is where speed dropped.
