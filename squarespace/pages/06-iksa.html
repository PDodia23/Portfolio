A tracker brand that explains itself in three verbs.

IKSA is a client’s brand of small, stick-on Bluetooth trackers. Before anyone can hold one, the website has to do all the selling. I designed the brand and a single landing page that takes a stranger from “what is this?” to joining the waiting list. The site is live now.

01 In brief

Small object, simple promise.

THE IDEA

Three verbs carry the whole brand, so anyone gets it in one read.

THE PROBLEM

Tracker brands lead with specs and networks. Buyers just want to stop losing their keys, and a brand that hasn’t launched has no reviews or shelf space to lean on.

02 Challenge

Every tracker sounds the same.

Product pages across the category talk about ecosystems, ranges and protocols. That is fine for early adopters and confusing for everyone else, especially people buying one for a parent or a child’s school bag.

05 Brand

Calm, warm and quietly confident.

Warm linen, daylight photography and one light typeface, PP Neue Montreal, keep it well away from gadget black and neon. The mark is a dot with signal waves either side, so the logo itself says “it finds things”. The product is still being made, so there was nothing real to film yet. I made the site's video with Higgsfield AI instead.

08 UX writing

Short verbs, no jargon.

The tagline sets the voice for the whole page. Headlines are short sentences anyone could say out loud, and the technical detail waits in the FAQ for the people who want it. I drafted the copy with help from Claude, then shaped it into this voice.

09 Details

Small things that make it feel considered.

A 100px promise with a soft tonal fade, an accordion FAQ, frosted cards that keep text readable over photos, and a waiting list that asks for one thing. Live at iksatracker.co.uk.

10 Options I rejected

What I left off the page.

Before launch, every extra choice is a reason to leave. These are the options I turned down, and what went on the page instead.

✕ Rejected

Spec tables and comparison charts

✓ Instead

Six sections that each answer one buyer question, with the technical detail waiting in the FAQ.

✕ Rejected

Asking for an account before launch

✓ Instead

Just an email address, asked for in two places: after the steps and at the end.

✕ Rejected

Longer Sanskrit names, like Smriti and Dhruva

✓ Instead

IKSA: four letters, and it means sight.

✕ Rejected

Detailed logo marks that blurred when small

✓ Instead

A dot with signal waves either side, so the logo itself says “it finds things”.

11 Reflection

Live, and early.

The site is live as a pre-launch brand site with an email waitlist, ahead of launch in early 2027. It is too early to claim results, so this shows the thinking: one job for the page, one voice, and a brand that looks like the moment you find something. What I can measure now is how well it is built.
