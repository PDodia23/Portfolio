I audited my own portfolio, then rebuilt it to WCAG 2.2 AA.

My old site looked bold but failed basics I design for every day: tiny grey text on black, headings used for styling, images without alt text and no way to contact me. I treated it like a client project: audit, prioritise, redesign, rebuild in code and measure.

01 · IN BRIEF

A portfolio that talked about accessibility, but did not practise it.

MY ROLE

Auditor, designer and developer. Every fix is mine, in Figma and in code.

APPROACH

Automated and manual audit, fix what blocks people first, redesign the type system, rebuild.

RESULT

On the coded build of the new home page: axe issues 7 → 0 in both light and dark themes, and Lighthouse accessibility 94 → 100 in the dark theme.

02 · THE AUDIT

What I found when I tested my own site.

I ran automated checks with Lighthouse and axe, then tested by hand with a keyboard, VoiceOver and a phone. Seven problems stood out, and most of them were ones no automated score would catch.

03 · PRIORITISE

Fix what blocks people first.

I ordered fixes by who they stop, not by how easy they are. A recruiter who cannot contact me is a bigger problem than an inconsistent spelling.

04 · REDESIGN

A type and colour system that passes by default.

Body text went from 10.2px to 16px on a clear scale. Every text colour was checked against black, and the one token that still fails is shown, not hidden.

STRUCTURE

One H1 per page, headings in order, and every case study tile labelled with a title, summary and role.

MOBILE DIAGRAMS

Complex diagrams open full screen and zoom, with a text summary underneath.

05 · BUILD

Rebuilt in code, not just restyled.

I am rebuilding it in Squarespace with hand-coded HTML, CSS and JavaScript in Code Blocks: semantic HTML, a skip link, visible focus states, Reduce Motion support and real alt text for every image. Components match one to one, so the pill in Figma is the pill in code.

06 · OUTCOME

Before and after.

I re-tested the new home page in November 2026, using my hand-coded build in a local preview before it goes into Squarespace. The first re-test took Lighthouse from 94 to 97 and axe from 7 issues to 6, all from one grey. Lifting that grey, plus a slightly darker orange in light mode, took axe to 0 issues in both themes, on desktop and mobile, and Lighthouse to 100 in the dark theme.

07 · OPTIONS I REJECTED

Options I rejected.

I treated my own site like a client project, which meant turning down the quick fixes.

✕ Rejected

Fixing the easiest issues first

✓ Instead

Fix what blocks people first: contact details, text size and contrast, broken headings.

✕ Rejected

Trusting the Lighthouse score

✓ Instead

The old site scored 94, which looks like a pass. I tested by hand too, with a keyboard, VoiceOver and a phone.

✕ Rejected

Restyling the old site

✓ Instead

Rebuilt in code: semantic HTML, a skip link, visible focus states and real alt text.

✕ Rejected

Hiding the faint grey that still failed

✓ Instead

Show it, fix it and re-test: lifting it took axe to 0 issues in both themes and Lighthouse to 100.

08 · REFLECTION

Auditing my own work was humbling.

The issues were the same ones I catch in other people’s designs, which is exactly why a checklist and real testing matter more than good intentions.
